The market for crystal and gemstone bracelets is full of dyed glass, reconstituted
powder, and plastic sold as the real thing. Here is how to tell a natural stone
from an imitation before you buy — and after your piece arrives.

## Feel the temperature

Natural stone is a poor conductor of heat. Held against the back of your hand or
your cheek, a genuine gemstone bead feels cool and takes a moment to warm up.
Glass and plastic warm almost instantly and feel "room temperature" from the
first touch.

## Look closely at the beads

Under good light, natural stones show small imperfections — faint veining,
cloudy patches, tiny internal fractures, and subtle colour shifts from bead to
bead. These are not flaws; they are the fingerprint of a stone that grew in the
earth. Perfectly uniform beads with identical colour and no inclusions are a
sign of manufactured material.

## Weigh it in your hand

Most natural gemstones are denser than glass and noticeably denser than plastic.
A bracelet that feels suspiciously light for its size is worth questioning.

## Be honest about price

Real, ethically sourced stone has a floor price. If a "natural citrine" or
"lapis lazuli" bracelet costs a fraction of everything else on the market, the
most likely explanation is that it isn't what the label says.

## Ask for certification

The most reliable signal is documentation. Every OJARA piece is a natural stone,
authenticated before it reaches you and dispatched as **lab-certified authentic**.
When a seller can't tell you where a stone came from or won't stand behind its
authenticity, treat that as your answer.

## A note on colour

Some natural stones are commonly heat-treated or stabilised — this is normal and
disclosed in the trade. What you want to avoid is *dyed* material passed off as
naturally coloured. If colour rubs off on a soft cloth or collects in the drill
holes of the beads, the stone has been dyed.

A genuine gemstone bracelet should feel cool, carry a little weight, show its
natural character, and come from someone willing to vouch for it. When all four
line up, you're wearing the real thing.
